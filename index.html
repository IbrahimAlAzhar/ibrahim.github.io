Ibrahim Al Azher

Ph.D. Researcher in Computer Science · Northern Illinois University · DATALab

Department of Computer Science

Northern Illinois University

DeKalb, IL, USA

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at Northern Illinois University (expected Aug. 2027), where I work in the DATALab with Dr. Hamed Alhoori. I build verifiable AI systems that expand, rather than narrow, scientific understanding.

My research sits at the intersection of AI for Science and Science of Science:

  • Evidence-grounded multi-agent reasoning — specialist LLM agents that retrieve, verify, and synthesize scholarly evidence to critique papers and surface research limitations and future directions (LimAgents, BAGELS, SciLimAgents).
  • Verifiable post-training — reinforcement learning with process-level, checkable rewards (SFT, DPO, step-attributed GRPO) instead of holistic LLM-judge scores (CiteSynth).
  • Preserving distinctive insight — measuring when machine aggregation discards valid but uncommon findings, and designing synthesis that keeps supported minority views.
  • Trustworthy AI-assisted judgment — prompt-injection attacks on LLM-assisted peer review, provenance-aware defenses, and mechanistic interpretability of evidence-grounded decisions (SciSec).

My work has appeared in EMNLP Findings, ACM/IEEE JCDL, IEEE BigData, IEEE DSAA, IEEE eScience, ACL and ICSE workshops. I received the 3MT Best Paper Presentation Award at JCDL 2024 and have received travel grants from NSF, SIGIR, IEEE, and MMLS, as well as Google Cloud research credits.

Before my Ph.D., I worked as a software engineer building ERP and e-commerce systems in Python/Odoo. I received my B.Sc. in Computer Science from Shahjalal University of Science and Technology, Bangladesh.

I am on the job market for postdoctoral and research scientist positions starting 2027. Feel free to reach out!

selected publications

  1. JCDL
    LimAgents: Limitation Generation of Scientific Article with LLM Agents and RAG
    Ibrahim Al Azher, Zhishuai Guo, and Hamed Alhoori
    In ACM/IEEE Joint Conference on Digital Libraries (JCDL). Dataset: Hugging Face , 2026
    Abs Bib Website

    A training-free multi-agent framework in which specialist, leader, and master agents critique a scientific article using citation retrieval and iterative feedback. Limitation-unit F1 improved from 37.91 to 52.36 on Mistral-7B and from 19.14 to 53.32 on Qwen2.5-3B over zero-shot baselines; feedback gains varied by model.

  2. EMNLP
    BAGELS: Benchmarking the Automated Generation and Extraction of Limitations from Scholarly Text
    Ibrahim Al Azher, Miftahul Jannat Mokarrama, Zhishuai Guo, Sagnik Ray Choudhury, and Hamed Alhoori
    In Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: EMNLP 2025. Dataset: Hugging Face , 2025
    Abs DOI arXiv Bib HTML Code

    Authors often underreport limitations and rely on hedging. We present a complete architecture for computational analysis of research limitations: a dataset of limitations extracted from papers and supplemented with external reviews, retrieval-augmented methods to generate limitations, and PointWise — a fine-grained evaluation that matches atomic Limitation Units rather than relying on paragraph-level similarity — together with a meta-evaluation.

  3. eScience
    FutureGen: A RAG-based Approach to Generate the Future Work of Scientific Article
    Ibrahim Al Azher, Miftahul Jannat Mokarrama, Zhishuai Guo, Sagnik Ray Choudhury, and Hamed Alhoori
    In AI4SC Workshop at IEEE eScience, 2025
    Abs arXiv Bib

    Retrieval-augmented future-work generation with iterative self-feedback. On ACL data, a two-iteration GPT-4o-mini RAG configuration reached 20.87 ROUGE-L versus 17.69 for one-pass generation.

  4. JCDL
    LimTopic: LLM-based Topic Modeling and Text Summarization for Analyzing Scientific Articles’ Limitations
    Ibrahim Al Azher, Venkata Devesh Reddy Seethi, Akhil Pandey Akella, and Hamed Alhoori
    In Proceedings of the 24th ACM/IEEE Joint Conference on Digital Libraries (JCDL), 2024
    Abs DOI arXiv Bib HTML Code

    Combines BERTopic with LLM-generated titles and summaries to organize limitation statements from scientific articles into interpretable topics and concise Topic Summaries. Few-shot GPT-4 achieved 0.617 coherence and 0.588 silhouette in the topic-plus-summary setting, versus 0.601 and 0.577 for BERTopic alone.
